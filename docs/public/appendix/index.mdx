# The Reckoning of the Reach

The first copy carried an Archive seal. The second disputed its dates. A third
arrived from a Thorn vessel with six pages on transfer law, three songs without
music, and a correction to the mass of Catenary's oldest wheel.

No council reconciled them. The present reckoning keeps their disagreements where
certainty would cost more truth than it supplied. Its account begins before the
roads, when distance still appeared to be an emptiness rather than a country with
laws of its own.

## Earth Before the Roads

Human beings entered the outward dark for practical reasons. They wanted sunlight
above weather, metals that had never lain beneath a nation, clocks far from a
crowded world's noise, and room for machines whose waste heat could open into
night. The first stations carried the severe names of projects. Their crews spoke
of rotations, completion bonuses, and the places to which they meant to return.

A temporary room needs air as urgently as a palace. Seals acquired patches.
Gardens took root in drums meant for freight. Children learned the difference
between a structural wall and a partition by the sound each gave beneath a
knuckle. Every year of successful repair made departure less certain.

The engines improved. They never made distance small. Mass still demanded
propellant. Propellant demanded tankage. Heat sought a radiator broad enough to
survive its own shadow. Ships crossed the new roads in weeks and months while
light carried a message ahead of them. Authority traveled at the speed of its
answer, and emergencies seldom waited.

The settlements became islands before anyone admitted there was an ocean.

## The Charter Years

The outward works grew under charters issued by governments, universities,
commercial houses, insurers, and labor trusts. A charter could promise a public
beacon in one paragraph and a private monopoly in the next. It named the owner of
a wheel, the keeper of a clock, the price of rescue, and the court that would hear
a dispute years after the people involved had solved it themselves.

Those years built greatly. Smelters opened among the small bodies. Fabrication
yards learned to turn local metal into pressure hulls. Optical references crossed
interplanetary distances. The first camps reached the dark moon later called
Sable, and separate wheels gathered near the Giant where transfer roads met.

The same years spent people freely. Crews signed for one season and inherited a
machine. Supply offices counted a repaired pump as proof that the hands beside it
could endure another delay. A distant seal remained legally perfect while the
organization impressed into it changed its name, sold its interest, and forgot
the corridor where the seal still carried weight.

Each great work entered the Reach with two histories: the purpose engraved on its
plate and the life made by those who kept it running.

## When the Ships Stopped Coming

Later accounts call the Long Withdrawal an event. People who lived through it
remember a sequence of absences.

A scheduled bearing arrived three months late. A replacement crew lost its
appropriation. A parent company merged with another whose registry had no field
for a moon on an irregular orbit. Warehouses filled with parts ordered for
machines already rebuilt by hand. Instructions continued after assistance ended.

No fleet turned back in a single season. The inner worlds faced their own repairs,
and the outward charters failed by degrees: debt, war, exhausted subsidies,
changing governments, ventures whose promised wealth had existed mainly in their
prospectuses. Every faction later selected the cause that made its own conduct
look inevitable.

The residents had less leisure for history. They took inventory. They opened
stores under witness, joined incompatible air systems, and assigned maintenance
that no surviving office could authorize. Some habitats purchased themselves for
one ceremonial unit of account. Some waited through every required hearing and
entered abandonment in local law. Others simply kept breathing until possession
became the oldest fact left.

The roads endured. Their government had changed hands.

## Air Made Law

The first pressure agreements were written during failures. Their language was
brief because the air was moving.

A person who entered a shared volume entered its obligations. Those beside a
failing wall could close it without awaiting an owner elsewhere. Stored mass,
power, water, and dangerous cargo had to answer to the people whose lives they
could alter. Salvage carried known debts forward. A common pipe joined duties
before it joined governments.

Ports copied the useful clauses and argued over the rest. In time the agreements
became the Covenant of Air, though no single parchment carries every accepted
form. Catenary reads the Covenant through residency. Sable reads it through
cooperative labor. The Archive reads it through durable obligation.

The Covenant displaced an older idea: that title alone conferred authority over a
habitat. Maintenance had become political. A person might inherit wealth or a
name, but the right to command a pressure door arose among those who shared the
consequence.

For this reason Catenary distinguishes birthplace from residency. A resident
accepts duties concerning air, mass, emergency work, and the cargo brought across
a seal. The city asks where a person stands when the wall fails.

## Roads of Light

Messages outrun ships throughout the Reach. They do not make every road equally
wide.

A narrow optical beam carries immense volumes while its bearings hold. Moon,
station, or weather can close the line. Microwave and radio survive rougher
conditions with less capacity. Near the Giant, charged fronts shift carriers by
frequency and disturb the clocks by smaller, treacherous amounts.

Thus a vessel may arrive after its own summary and before the full meaning of that
summary. Compact manifests cross by radio. Raw observations, calibration trails,
machine receipts, replacement parts, and private correspondence travel in hulls.
A packet ship carries the weight behind a number.

Orbital geometry supplies seasons. Radiation fronts supply weather. Propellant
marks the length of a pilot's patience. A missed road may return in hours, months,
or after the cargo has lost its use.

The common beacons are therefore more than lamps. They are agreements about time,
frequency, correction, and whose observation may alter a road used by everyone.
The present labor of joining those agreements is called the Second Navigation.
Its promise is a shared sky. Its danger is that a shared standard may acquire an
owner.

## Catenary

Catenary was assembled before it was founded.

One wheel carried smelters and the people who slept beside them. Another grew food
under lamps tuned to an earthly noon. Construction yards accumulated berths,
machine shops, markets, schools, and pressure corridors that crossed from one
charter into another. Their spin rates disagreed. Utility pipes met anyway.

Traffic law became the city's first common speech. Fire response followed, then
water accounts, dock mass, and the arbitration of repairs whose consequences
crossed a seal. District councils retained their old powers. A civic assembly
grew in the spaces between them.

From a distance the wheels appear braided. Within them, gravity changes along a
walk, breakfast belongs to three shifts, and a public wall carries whatever later
residents could persuade it to bear. Neighborhoods received air before they
received names.

Yard Nine lies on an old construction wheel where finished ships inherit soot
from unfinished work. The city considers its inconvenience part of the rent.
Workers consider the rent evidence that inconvenience has an owner after all.

Nobody needs to have been born in Catenary to call it home. Home there is a
continuing account: duties accepted, doors opened, weight taken, and a place kept
for one's return without any promise that the place will remain unchanged.

## The Black Vault

The Archive began as neutral ground among charter powers that trusted one another
only in duplicate. Cargo titles, identities, engineering revisions, debts,
marriages, inheritances, and accident trails entered its stores. Rival owners
accepted the Archive because no house would entrust its history to another.

Its inhabited rim shines softly around a central vault built to shed little heat.
A ship receives storage before docking instructions. Citizens inherit no land.
They inherit keys, obligations, and the witnessed choice to preserve or destroy
what the dead entrusted to them.

During an evacuation a century ago, the repair controller destroyed its sole
recoverable pressure model. Engineers saved the habitat. A sealed compartment held twelve workers who died.

The custodians answered by requiring complete transition histories from every
critical machine.
They carved the answer into their public protocols:

```text
WHAT IS REMEMBERED CAN BE RETURNED
```

The words saved later lives. They also gathered meanings their makers had not
settled. A machine can be rewound through stored history. An operation may possess
an inverse requiring no private journal. A room can return to service while its
dead remain dead. The Archive's wisest custodians now preserve the noun beside
the verb.

Its records give the Reach continuity across vanished companies and divided
ports. Its errors endure with equal patience.

## Sable at the Weatherward Edge

Sable follows an irregular captured path at the sunward boundary of the Giant's
magnetic field. Early surveyors mistook its darkness for missing data. Later crews
found pale veins beneath the black surface and gave each excavation a name useful
at the end of a cable.

The sunward camp was Dawn, and Evening traveled the opposing rim. Cross kept the
surface road. Deep ceased its migration after the lava-tube refuge opened.

The first camps carried navigation clocks. Their keepers compared oscillators by
walking them through tunnels in sealed cases, and every comparison became a
meeting. Radiation storms erased three years' labor on the exposed surface. The camps moved
underground, where old words survived new instruments.

Charter ventures came with drills, promises, and debts. Several pressure shells
outlasted the businesses that sold them. When the sponsors withdrew, the
remaining residents formed the Sable Instrument Cooperative. They honored the
camp debts because abandonment by one party did not erase labor performed by the
other.

The far instrument stands in Sable's orbit. Its chambers lie far enough apart
along the trusses that warmth from one refrigerator cannot reach them all. It compares optical clocks, microwave
carriers, local time, magnetic change, and charged weather. Its quantum machinery
measures phase with a precision for which the older camps had no language. Its
pumps still answer to hands carrying their maker's marks.

Sable meets some storms before Catenary. Distance turns the small station into a
weather shore for the inner traffic lanes. Five people may keep watch there while
thousands travel under the correction their work supports.

## The Families Between Ports

During the Withdrawal, transport crews found themselves aboard vessels owned by
companies that had become addresses for unanswered notices. Some fleets were
seized by creditors. Others entered worker trusts. A few kept moving until their
right of possession had crossed every court that might have disputed it.

The Thorn compact began among such ships. Its white mark first told approaching
salvage crews that life and lawful custody continued aboard. The mark became a
sign of approach rights, shared repair, and kinship carried across jurisdictions.

A Thorn address begins with the vessel, narrows to a pressure section, and ends
with the transfers its people expect to make. Children learn relative motion before municipal geography. Home begins
when two locks match velocity well enough to meet.

Many Thorn people later take residency in ports. Others remain aboard flotillas.
The compact offers mobility rather than innocence: its members inherit unpaid
accounts, maintenance disputes, old loyalties, and the knowledge that every
family vessel will someday need a yard belonging to someone else.

Their pilots brought a maritime grammar into the Second Navigation. A road is
shared through conduct. A signal matters because another vessel trusts it with
mass.

## Wheeler and the Work of Return

The first outward machines treated heat as an engineering inconvenience. Closed
habitats made it an account. Every computation warmed something. Every erased
distinction sent its price toward a radiator.

Reversible logic grew from that pressure and from a second need. Critical systems
had to explain how one state followed another. Event histories supported recovery
through failure. Independently invertible operations restored selected state after
a history closed. Quantum controls required coherent transformations, generated
adjoints, and a visible boundary at measurement.

Wheeler joined these practices without blending them.

A rewind follows retained history. An inverse performs a transformation whose
outputs identify their inputs. An adjoint reverses a unitary operation while
coherence survives. Replay feeds a saved observation through later classical
work. Retry asks physics for another observation. Each road may reach a familiar
number and still carry a different authority.

Shipboard copies of early lessons traveled farther than their maintainers. Crews
added programs, repairs, proofs, accident accounts, and corrections in the
margins. The family of volumes became known as the Common Book of Return. No
accepted author stands at its beginning.

One rule appears in several recensions:

```text
Name no distinction before an experiment requires it.
Retain every name that survives the experiment.
```

A gray cloth copy aboard *Vela* carries hands from the Archive, Sable, Catenary,
and voyages whose names have worn away. Its authority comes afresh whenever an
experiment supports the ink.

## Calendar of the Reach

No calendar governs every port. Catenary counts civic years from the ratification
of its common traffic articles. Sable keeps cooperative years and several older
camp seasons. The Archive can translate among them, though its translations have
occasionally outlived the governments that requested them.

The shared historical order is simpler:

| Era | What endured |
| --- | --- |
| Earth Before the Roads | orbital fabrication, closed habitats, precision clocks |
| Charter Years | the first wheels, camps, bearings, and clearinghouses |
| Long Withdrawal | local possession, interrupted supply, inherited obligations |
| Covenant of Air | resident authority and duties across shared pressure |
| Second Navigation | common beacons and evidence among autonomous ports |

People date ordinary life by departures, arrivals, maintenance seasons, and the
storms that altered both. A family may remember that a child was born during the
west shutter failure more readily than the corresponding civic year.

## Names, Offices, and Measures

Names in the Reach often begin as instructions. Catenary describes structures
joined across open distance. Sable records appearance. Dawn, Cross, Evening, and
Deep once told camp crews where to carry a clock. A useful name survives the
machine that first required it.

An office identifies a duty before it confers honor. A pilot answers for the
physical road. A keeper answers for an instrument and its dependencies. A
custodian answers for retained material. A traffic director answers for the
continuity of many vessels whose crews will never meet.

Measures retain their ordinary physical meanings. Distance does not become a
metaphor when a ship is short of propellant. An amplitude is not an event count.
A proof does not certify a warm bearing. The language of the Reach has grown
careful because its grandest failures often began with one word asked to carry
several kinds of truth.

The roads remain open. Their lights belong to everyone who accepts the work of
keeping them so.
