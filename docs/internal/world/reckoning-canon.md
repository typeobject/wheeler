# Canon for the Reach

This file governs the setting of *Home Was the Easy Part* and its public appendix,
*The Reckoning of the Reach*. The public history may preserve uncertainty. The
novella may reveal a fact through action. Neither should contradict the fixed
points gathered here.

## Governing premise

Humanity learned to build durable islands in space before it learned to make
distance cheap.

The Reach is a settled region around a giant planet in the outer system. Its
people possess mature orbital industry, closed habitats, precise clocks,
reversible computation, and specialized quantum instruments. Their ships still
pay for every change of velocity. Journeys follow orbital geometry and take days
or weeks. Radiation fronts alter routes and close exposed work. Light outruns all
vessels, while distance and fragile bearings keep authority local.

This is an Age of Sail created by physics and history. It does not reproduce the
clothes or speech of an earthly century. Its ports, packet ships, observatories,
migrant vessel families, work guilds, sealed manifests, and navigational commons
grew from the same conditions that once made oceans politically large.

## The shape of the Reach

### The Giant

The settled region circles one giant planet. In ordinary speech it is the Giant.
Formal ephemerides carry an older astronomical name that the novella does not
need. Its magnetic field catches the star's charged wind and draws storms into
vast moving fronts. Aurora follows the field inward. Timing, microwave travel,
particle density, and safe exposure change as the weather crosses the system.

The Giant supplies no convenient surface. Human life occupies wheels, trusses,
mines, shielded camps, vessels, and the altered cavities of small moons.

### Catenary

Catenary turns close to the Giant, among the useful transfer orbits. It began as
separate charter works: smelters, construction wheels, gardens, freight yards,
crew quarters, and navigation stations. Their sponsors selected different spin
rates and engineering standards. Pipes and traffic agreements joined them before
streets did.

The city is now a federation of old wheels, younger habitats, yards, and districts
whose borders remain legible in pressure doors and utility accounts. No single
architect planned its silhouette. Residents call this evidence of freedom,
neglect, or successful repair according to the meeting.

Catenary governs shared air, traffic, dock mass, emergency power, and the beacons
used by approaching ships. Districts retain authority over many domestic and
commercial matters. Residency carries obligations because a person crossing a
pressure seal joins a physical commons. Birth elsewhere does not weaken
residency. Birth inside Catenary does not excuse anyone from its duties.

Yard Nine is an old construction berth on a less fashionable wheel. Its gravity
changes along connected corridors. Public walls carry lawful structure and the
attachments later residents needed.

### The Archive

The Archive occupies a stable corridor reached early on the outward road from
Catenary. Its inhabited rim surrounds a cold central vault. Ships encounter its
jurisdiction and storage offers before they see its lights.

It began during the Charter Years as a clearinghouse. Rival sponsors needed a
neutral keeper for cargo title, identity, engineering revisions, insurance,
inheritance, and arbitration. Storage became citizenship. The Archive survived
because each power preferred a neutral ledger to a rival's memory.

A century before the novella, an automated repair system overwrote the only
machine state from which a pressure model could be reconstructed during an
evacuation. The habitat survived. Twelve workers behind a sealed door died. The
custodians answered by retaining every transition in every critical machine and
by engraving their policy into public speech: `WHAT IS REMEMBERED CAN BE
RETURNED`.

The policy prevented later losses and slowly acquired the force of doctrine.
Sana's dispute with Edrin concerns the moral boundary of that doctrine. A perfect
machine history may still describe human loss inside a frame too narrow to be
honest.

### Sable

Sable is a dark captured moon on a wide, irregular orbit near the sunward edge of
the Giant's magnetic weather. It often meets a charged front before Catenary.
Early surveys mistook its shadow for absent data. Pale mineral veins appear where
prospecting charges cut the black surface.

The first camps anchored clocks and compared navigation signals. Later sponsors
added mining trials, timing cavities, and the far instrument. Three years of
exposed work vanished in radiation storms. The settlements moved into tunnels
and shield-shadow. Several commercial schemes failed, leaving pressure shells,
debts, and machines behind.

When the charter sponsors withdrew, the people who remained formed the Sable
Instrument Cooperative. The cooperative honors camp obligations that the former
owners abandoned. Five people live at the far instrument during the novella.
They share machine work, water accounts, bandwidth, food cultivation, and target
watch. Iona Vale is the instrument's keeper, not its sovereign.

The far instrument compares Catenary's optical clock reference, microwave
navigation carriers, Sable's local clocks, and the charged environment. Its
quantum target measures coherent phase. Classical reducers combine those
observations with carrier delay, particle counts, magnetic readings, and
calibration data. The instrument informs a correction. It does not choose one.

### Roads and communications

No signal travels faster than light. Local occultations and delay give every
habitat authority during emergencies because remote permission arrives after the
moment of choice.

Narrow optical links carry scheduled high-volume traffic when geometry and
bearings permit. Microwave and radio survive more conditions at lower capacity.
Charged weather alters propagation. Instruments can produce raw data faster than
the reliable fallback channels can carry it.

Packet vessels therefore transport equipment, bulk archives, physical samples,
and the full ancestry of compact results. A radio manifest can arrive before a
ship without containing everything needed for final acceptance. The division is
ordinary and legally important.

Orbital transfer windows, propellant, heat rejection, traffic, and radiation
weather play the part once played by winds and seasons. Pilots do not command the
weather. They preserve choices until a safe road remains.

## Historical reckoning

The Reach uses several local calendars. The relative chronology below is canon.
Public dates may be given as generations or in Catenary civic years.

### Earth Before the Roads

Humanity developed reusable launch, orbital fabrication, improved closed-loop
life support, and high-precision metrology during a long response to terrestrial
energy and climate pressures. Settlement began as infrastructure. Power crews,
clock keepers, surveyors, fabricators, and miners expected contracts followed by
rotation home.

Improved drives lowered cost without abolishing travel time. Habitats became
durable before sponsors could govern them closely. Families formed in places
built as temporary works.

### The Charter Years

Public authorities, universities, commercial houses, insurers, and labor trusts
financed expansion toward the Giant. Their charters joined ownership, public
purpose, and monopoly privileges in unstable combinations.

The charter fleets built the first elements of Catenary, the Archive clearinghouse,
Sable's camps, and the bearing network. Remote owners treated workers as
replaceable crews. Distance made replacement expensive, while practical knowledge
accumulated among those who stayed.

The period produced great works and durable injuries. Routes, standards, and
habitats survived. So did debt labor, abandoned obligations, incompatible
interfaces, and legal titles held by institutions that had ceased to maintain the
property they claimed.

### The Long Withdrawal

Expansion capital receded over many years. No single catastrophe explains it.
Inner-system reconstruction, political fracture, exhausted subsidies, failed
ventures, and changing priorities all contributed. Later factions condensed the
era into one betrayal because a single enemy was easier to inherit.

Supply schedules thinned. Crew rotations failed. Parent organizations merged,
dissolved, or continued issuing commands after they had lost the ability to help.
The roads remained open, but nobody could promise that the next ship belonged to
the same order as the last.

Maintenance crews and residents took possession in practice. Some transitions
were negotiated, some purchased for symbolic sums, and some entered in local law
after the owner missed every hearing. The Archive preserved conflicting titles
until new compacts gave them meaning.

### Air Made Law

The pressure settlements began as emergency agreements. Anyone sharing a sealed
volume shared its immediate hazards and the labor of preserving it. Those
agreements became the Covenant of Air, with local forms across the Reach.

The Covenant established broad principles:

- maintained habitation creates obligations that absentee title cannot discharge.
- emergency authority belongs near the physical system at risk.
- residents account for mass, air, power, and dangerous cargo crossing a seal.
- salvage does not erase known debt or authorship.
- a common service may join jurisdictions without making them one polity.
- records supporting public danger or public obligation must preserve their
  parentage.

Catenary's residency law, Sable's cooperative charter, and the Archive's neutral
standing descend from different readings of the Covenant.

### The Second Navigation

The withdrawal left autonomous ports with drifting standards. Trade continued
through local bargains, inherited clocks, and careful pilots. As traffic grew,
those arrangements became dangerous.

The current era seeks common beacons, calibration, package identities, and rules
of evidence across independent settlements. People call it the Second Navigation.
Its central political question is whether coordination can grow without restoring
remote ownership.

Catenary's beacon program belongs to this effort. Accepting Sable's calibration
recognizes the cooperative as a source of physical evidence within the shared
network. Rejecting weak evidence protects traffic. Rejecting sound evidence
because it came from a distant small station would turn a common standard into a
metropolitan monopoly.

The novella occurs while that question remains open.

## Material and technical culture

### Scarcity

The Reach has capable fabrication, but no universal matter printer. Feedstock,
trace elements, certified components, biological stock, propellant, radiator
area, machine time, and skilled labor remain scarce. A reused allocation refunds
space, not energy already spent. A repaired ship carries the decisions of every
shop that kept it alive.

Heat is an account. Computation, habitation, and industry all produce it. This
made low-energy and reversible methods valuable long before quantum control made
them intellectually fashionable.

### Ships

Ships are assembled for long service and survive changes of owner and purpose.
Standard interfaces help. Old hulls still demand local knowledge. *Vela* began as
a survey tender. Her port radiator came from a hospital barge. Her maneuvering
model came from a racing tug. She is legally one vessel and materially a history
of agreements.

A pilot's authority concerns mass, velocity, corridor, fuel, and the physical
consequences of delay. A pilot does not certify a scientific result merely by
carrying it.

### Computation

High-risk software favors visible state, reproducible artifacts, explicit limits,
and named effects. Opaque predictive systems exist, especially in weather and
maintenance, but they cannot become the final signer for an irreversible public
action. The transit failure in Tala's past strengthened this practice at
Catenary.

Wheeler grew where three engineering traditions met:

- reversible classical work developed to reduce heat and make transformations
  independently invertible.
- event histories supported recovery and accountability across failures.
- quantum control required unitary operations, generated adjoints, coherent use,
  measurement boundaries, and target planning.

Wheeler keeps these roads separate in one language. Rewind spends retained
history. A generated inverse performs new work. A unitary adjoint reverses a
coherent operation before measurement. Replay applies a saved observation to
later classical work. Retry creates another physical experiment.

### Quantum instruments

Quantum machines remain physical instruments with pumps, refrigeration, control
fields, calibration, queues, and error. Most people encounter quantum work through
clocks, materials, sensors, or public infrastructure rather than general-purpose
consumer machines.

An ideal simulator may expose its numerical state vector. Hardware returns
observations, timing, calibration, and uncertainty. Proofs address mathematical
propositions under stated assumptions. None substitutes for the others.

### Evidence

A digest identifies bytes. Lineage explains how those bytes arose. Public work
may require source, artifact, plan, target receipt, observation, reducer,
calibration, and certificate, each carrying its own authority.

The Archive taught the Reach to preserve trails. The Covenant taught it that a
trail serves people only when its frame includes the physical obligations at
stake.

## People and offices

### Tala

Tala spent seven years analyzing Catenary transit systems. She halted restoration
when a copied control state omitted fourteen cars under manual power. Her duty is
to identify the experiment and the scope of its answer. Under pressure she can
continue dividing a question after action has become necessary.

She joins *Vela* as systems analyst at the start of the novella. The gray manual
becomes her route into Wheeler and quantum computation.

### Mara

Mara pilots *Vela*. She grew up among the Thorn Families, where home meant a
matched velocity and a lock that could open. Catenary became her first address
that remained valid while she traveled. At departure, a vessel on which she
learned docking offers a berth through its next transfer. After returning, Mara
answers with Catenary's moving coordinates and a reciprocal berth offer.

Her authority covers the ship's physical road. She preserves fuel and decision
windows, and she accepts qualified action when delay creates greater danger. Her
weakness is impatience with distinctions whose physical cost has not yet reached
her console.

### Osei

Osei is responsible for machinery, restoration, and resource use. He worked at
Sable and left for a refit at Catenary six years before the novella. The promised
year filled with jobs he chose. Iona stopped reserving a future neither controlled.

He distrusts state that cannot explain its earlier form. He can mistake clean
workspace for a completed human obligation.

### Sana

Sana trained under Edrin Saye at the Archive. She left after their dispute over an
accurate evacuation history framed around restored machinery while twelve deaths
became an overlay.

She keeps distinctions and the parentage of results aboard *Vela*. Her standards
protect the mission. They can also consume the time in which evidence must become
a decision.

### Iona Vale

Iona keeps the far instrument and answers to Sable's cooperative. She understands
its mathematics, controls, weather, maintenance debts, and civic cost. She and
Osei loved each other before distance became a life neither had agreed to live.

She guards the authority of physical observation and local knowledge. That duty
can make every distant summary look thinner than the decision it must support.

### Neris Venn

Venn directs traffic continuity at Catenary. During Tala's transit failure she
reopened a copied green state to protect crowded platforms. Tala's veto exposed
the cars outside that copy. Venn later wrote an honest warning that helped Tala
join the mission.

Venn must make incomplete evidence serve a whole population. She represents the
necessary scale of federation and its recurring temptation to compress local
exceptions out of view. At *Vela*'s final hold, she spends public traffic capacity
to give the crew time, then acknowledges privately that she remembers the
fourteen cars.

### Edrin Saye

Edrin is an Archive custodian and Sana's former teacher. He once defended a
technically accurate exhibit beyond the point of honesty. His later amendments
show that institutions can learn without pretending the original harm has been
reversed.

## Vessel families and the Thorn compact

During the withdrawal, transport crews found themselves aboard ships whose owners
had vanished into mergers and courts. Several fleets placed their vessels in
family and labor trusts. The Thorn compact was one of them. Its painted white mark
began as a salvage warning and became a sign of mutual approach rights.

Thorn addresses name a vessel, a pressure section, and planned transfers. Children
learn locks, manifests, and relative motion early. Some remain mobile. Others hold
residency in ports without surrendering fleet kinship. The families are neither a
separate species nor a romantic escape from civic life. They carry debts,
conflicts, and maintenance obligations across jurisdictions.

## The Common Book and the gray recension

During the Charter Years, crews copied useful programs, proofs, failure accounts,
propellant arithmetic, and local corrections into shipboard books. Copies diverged
as vessels traveled. The family of texts became known informally as the Common
Book of Return.

No accepted author exists. Some leaves descend from a training manual. Others
from accident reports, union lessons, or private notebooks. The work has authority
only where an experiment, proof, or surviving practice supports it.

The gray cloth volume aboard *Vela* is one recension. Its oldest title page names
no author. Sana's paper tags, Osei's sailcloth repair, Iona's wiring route, and
Tala's later corrections join earlier hands. Its rule is:

```text
Name no distinction before an experiment requires it.
Retain every name that survives the experiment.
```

The public *Reckoning* describes the culture that made such books. It is not the
same document.

## Naming and speech

Place names usually began as work language. Catenary describes joined structures.
Sable describes appearance. Dawn, Cross, Evening, and Deep were camp directions
before they became calibration channels. Names persist when their first machinery
does not.

Formal documents use personal names, office, habitat residency, and artifact
identity as needed. The novella does not map names to surviving terrestrial
nations. Human cultures continued and mixed. Names alone assign no single modern
nationality.

Technical vocabulary remains standard: bit, inverse, amplitude, phase, qubit,
entanglement, unitary, adjoint, oracle, QFT, measurement, replay, retry. Local
metaphors may lead into these terms and must not replace them.

## Fixed continuity points

- The complete mission from departure to failed homecoming spans thirty-seven
  days.
- The Archive is encountered outbound before Sable.
- Sable may occult Catenary's signal. Local decisions continue during the gap.
- The charged front reaches Sable before Catenary in this season.
- The west bearing can retain radio while losing the narrow outbound laser.
- The compact mission manifest reaches Catenary before *Vela*. The full chain
  travels aboard the ship.
- At Sable, Iona uses an unprotected phase-estimation qualification, the Bell
  diagnostic, replay, and retry to plan a protected production comparison. The
  compact manifest describes the production observation.
- The initial Bell run measures `3`. Later evidence must never describe that
  measured state as restored.
- The generated adjoint may follow the unmeasured Bell preparation and return it
  to `|00>` in the ideal model.
- The inherited QFT can reverse its own incorrect gate order. Forward amplitude
  checks expose the error.
- The final burn is a new physical event. Replay cannot provide propulsion.
- Catenary changes while the crew travels. Home is continuing membership, not a
  preserved earlier arrangement.

## Questions intentionally left open

- The Giant's formal astronomical name.
- A single definitive cause or date for the Long Withdrawal.
- The identity of the first author represented in the Common Book.
- Whether the Second Navigation becomes a federation, a technical compact, or a
  new instrument of metropolitan control.
- Whether Osei ever returns to live at Sable.
- Whether Sana rejoins the Archive as a citizen. Correspondence alone does not
  settle it.
- Whether the Thorn Families eventually accept one permanent legal home.

Later work may answer these questions only through an explicit canon revision.
